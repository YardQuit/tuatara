* The screen

What Tuatara shows you, and the name of each part.

When Tuatara starts, its screen is divided into a few parts. Each part has a
name, and the rest of this manual uses those names. This chapter goes through
them from the large ones to the small ones. For each part it lists the keys
that act on it and the settings that change how it looks.

This manual is written with modal editing on, first and foremost. Tuatara has
it on until you turn it off, so the keys the manual gives first are those of
NORMAL state. Where a command also has a key that works with modal editing off,
the manual gives that too.

** Keys and names

Most commands have a key. A key written as ~C-x~ means hold down the Control
key and press x. A key written as ~M-x~ means hold down Alt (called Meta in
older manuals) and press x. On a terminal where Alt does not reach the
program, press Escape, let go, then press x. ~S-~ stands for Shift. On most
European keyboards the right Alt key is AltGr, and with a letter it works as
Meta too: AltGr-x is ~M-x~ (chapter 8 tells more).

Several keys in a row, as in ~C-x C-f~, are pressed one after the other. You
may keep Control held down through the whole sequence. A few keys have names
instead of letters: ~RET~ is Return (Enter), ~TAB~ is Tab, ~SPC~ is the space
bar, ~DEL~ is the Backspace key and ~ESC~ is Escape.

Every command also has a name, such as =find-file=. You can run any command by
its name. Press ~M-x~, type a few letters of the name, and press ~RET~. The
list of choices that appears shows the keys of each command beside it. A
command that has no key can only be run this way, and the tables in this
manual say ~M-x~ and its name for it.

A setting is a name that starts and ends with a star, such as
=*mode-line-tuatara*=. Settings go in your init file, the file Tuatara reads
when it starts, as in =(setf *mode-line-tuatara* nil)=. Each table of settings
in this chapter gives the default, the value Tuatara has until you change it.
~C-h v~ shows a setting's value, the values it takes and what each does.

Tuatara is a modal editor, which means a key can do different things
depending on a state the editor is in. There are two states you will meet
first. In NORMAL state, the letter keys are commands: ~i~ starts INSERT
state. In INSERT state, the letter keys write text. ~C-g~ or ~ESC~ takes you
back to NORMAL state. The mode line, described below, always tells you which
state you are in.

The tables of commands have two columns of keys. The Modal column holds the
keys of the modal layer, such as the letter keys of NORMAL state. They work only
with modal editing on. The Non-modal column holds the keys that work in both
states and with modal editing off: those with Control or Alt, the arrow keys
and the keys of a major mode. A side with no key says "none". In the text, a
key that has a partner of the other kind is followed by it in brackets, as in
~j~ (non-modal: ~C-n~) and ~C-n~ (modal: ~j~). To turn modal editing off, put
=(setf *modal-editing* nil)= in your init file. Every key of the Non-modal
column then does what the table says, and typing inserts text everywhere.

Most letter keys of NORMAL state that have no Control or Alt key of their own
are also under ~C-z~, in every state: ~m w~ selects a word, and ~C-z m w~ does
the same with modal editing off. The Non-modal column gives them, as ~C-z m w~.
A command that has a key in both states, such as ~w~ (non-modal: ~M-f~), is not
under ~C-z~ a second time.

The frame is everything Tuatara draws. In a window of its own it is that
window, and in a terminal it is the terminal's screen.

A window is a part of the frame that shows one piece of text. Each window has
a mode line under it, a row that describes what the window shows.

The echo area is the last row of the frame. Tuatara says short things there,
such as =Wrote /home/you/list.txt=.

The minibuffer is the row where Tuatara asks you a question. The question is
called the prompt, and what you type after it is your answer. While a question
is open, the minibuffer and the panel take the bottom of the frame in place of
the echo area: the minibuffer on top and the choices under it, or the
minibuffer on the last row when =*prompt-above-candidates*= is =nil=.

The panel is the rows at the bottom of the frame that list choices while a
question is open, and the keys you can press next after a key such as ~C-x~,
above the echo area. It is only there when it has something to say.

The cursor is the mark that shows where typed text goes. Other editors call
that place the point, and Tuatara's help uses the word too.

The gutter is the narrow strip at the left edge of a window. It can hold line
numbers.

** Windows and buffers

A buffer is a piece of text Tuatara holds in memory. When you open a file,
Tuatara reads it into a buffer. Your changes go to the buffer, and the file on
disk changes only when you save with ~C-x C-s~. Not every buffer has a file.
=*Messages*=, the log of what the echo area said, is a buffer. So is the list of
files you get from =find-file=. Buffers that Tuatara makes itself have their
names between stars.

A window is a view of one buffer. Two windows may show the same buffer, each
at its own place, or different buffers. Closing a window never closes its
buffer, and killing a buffer closes only the windows that were made for it.

Tuatara puts new windows where you would expect. A help buffer opens in
another window. The =*Messages*= log opens across the bottom, 30% of the
height, and takes the cursor so that you can read it; ~q~ closes it. A window
that already holds a buffer is never split into pieces smaller than
=*window-min-height*= rows or =*window-min-width*= columns.

#+begin_try Try it, with a shopping list and a recipe
1. Press ~C-x C-f~, type =~/shopping.txt= and press ~RET~. The file does not
   exist yet, so Tuatara makes an empty buffer with that name.
2. Press ~i~ to start INSERT state. Type =eggs=, ~RET~, =bread=, ~RET~, =milk=.
   Press ~C-g~ to go back to NORMAL state, then ~C-x C-s~ to save.
3. Press ~C-x 3~. The window splits in two, side by side, and both show the
   shopping list. The left window keeps the cursor.
4. Press ~C-x o~ to move to the right window. Press ~C-x C-f~, type
   =~/pancakes.txt=, press ~RET~, then press ~i~ and type a recipe.
5. Press ~C-x o~ to go back to the left window. Press ~C-x 1~. The left window
   fills the frame again. The pancake buffer is still there: press ~C-x b~,
   type =pan= and press ~RET~ to bring it back.
#+end_try

Each of the four keys that make a window larger or smaller says in the echo
area what share of the split every window has now, in per cent. Side by side
it reads =Widths, left to right: 52%, 48%=, and one above the other it reads
=Heights, top to bottom: 55%, 45%=. The shares may not add up to exactly 100,
as each is rounded. The echo area then lists the keys that repeat the change.
Right after one of the size commands, you can press a key alone to go on
resizing. ~}~ and ~>~ make the window wider, ~{~ and ~<~ narrower, ~^~ and ~+~
taller, and ~v~ and ~-~ shorter. If the window has no neighbour in that
direction, the echo area says that it cannot be made larger or smaller. With
the mouse,
drag a mode line up or down, or the line between two windows left or right.

** Frames, windows of their own and terminals

Tuatara runs in two ways. Typed as =tuatara=, it runs in the terminal you
started it from. Typed as =tuatara --gui=, it opens a window of its own on the
desktop, drawn with GTK, the toolkit most Linux desktops use. Both have the
same keys, the same commands and the same parts. The window of its own has
things a terminal does not, such as pictures in every case, a font you can
change and ligatures where the font has them.

A frame is one such screen. You can have several frames. In a window of its
own each frame is a separate desktop window. A terminal has only one screen,
so its frames take turns: the one you work in is shown, and the others wait.
When frames take turns, the mode line starts the buffer's name with the
frame's name, F1, F2 and so on. Every frame has its own windows and its own
echo area. They all show the same buffers, so a change you make in one frame
is in the other at once.

Tuatara can also run in the background as a daemon, started with
=tuatara --daemon=, and be reached with =tuatara --client=, each client with
frames of its own. The sign D in the mode line, described below, says that the
editor you are in runs as a daemon.

#+begin_try Try it, with two frames
1. In a terminal, start Tuatara with =tuatara ~/shopping.txt=.
2. Press ~C-x 5 2~. A second frame opens showing the same file. In a terminal
   the new frame replaces the first on the screen, and the mode line now
   starts the buffer's name with =F2=.
3. Press ~C-x 5 o~ to go to the other frame, and back again with the same
   keys. The mode line says =F1= in the first.
4. Press ~C-x 5 0~ in the second frame to close it. Closing the last frame is
   refused. Quit with ~C-x C-c~.
#+end_try

** The mode line

The mode line is the bar under every window. It says what the window shows
and in what state. The mode line of the window you are working in is drawn
brighter than the others. Here is a typical one.

#+attr_html: :screen t :ml 2 :signs PD :lit P
#+caption: Figure 1.2. A mode line. The sign P is lit: a job is running.
#+begin_example
Dear Hana, thank you for the lovely weekend.
 TUATARA[N] PD [-] letter.txt  L1 C0  Text  ~/letters/
#+end_example

Read left to right, the parts are these.

1. The modal state, in square brackets after the word TUATARA.
2. Up to four signs, P, U, S and D, always in the same place.
3. The marker that says whether the buffer is saved, [-], [*] or [%].
4. Words about the buffer itself, such as Safe or Narrow, when they apply.
5. The buffer's name.
6. The line and the column of the cursor.
7. The name of the buffer's major mode, when it has one.
8. Anything more the buffer has to say, such as CRLF or Page 3 of 10.
9. The directory the buffer lives in, at the right end.

Only the parts that apply are shown, and a mode line that is too narrow cuts
the buffer's name in the middle, with a mark for what is left out.

*** The modal state

The first part tells you what a key will do. =TUATARA[N]= is NORMAL state,
=TUATARA[I]= is INSERT state. =TUATARA[S]= is a support buffer, one that holds
a list, help or the =*Messages*= log. There you can read, move and select but
not type. =TUATARA[E]= is a buffer that holds no modal keys, such as the Lisp
prompt (IELM). With =*modal-editing*= set to =nil= there is no
modal part at all.

With =*mode-line-tuatara*= set to =nil= the word TUATARA is left out and only
=[N]= remains. ~C-x T t~ switches it while Tuatara runs.

*** The signs P, U, S and D

Four letters stand before the saved marker, side by side. Each is faint when
nothing is going on and bright, in the warning colour, while its work goes on.
They never move, so nothing after them jumps when one lights up. A sign stays
lit for half a second after its work ends, so that a short job can be seen.

| Sign | Setting                  | What it says                                                                                                                                                                          |
|------+--------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| P    | =*processing-indicator*= | Processing. A job is running for this buffer, or waits its turn. Jobs are things done in the background, such as a search through a large file, a sort or a save. ~C-x j~ lists them. |
| U    | =*indexing-indicator*=   | Update. A directory you asked Tuatara to keep read in memory is being read.                                                                                                           |
| S    | =*searching-indicator*=  | Search. A search runs over such a directory.                                                                                                                                          |
| D    | =*daemon-indicator*=     | Daemon. This editor runs in the background as a daemon.                                                                                                                               |

U and S only appear when =*indexed-directories*= names some directory, and P and
D always appear. Set any of the four settings to =nil= to hide that sign, or to
another piece of text, such as ="indexing"= or an icon from a font that has one,
to show that instead.

*** Saved or not

Next comes the marker for the buffer's state.

| Marker | Meaning                                                           |
|--------+-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| [-]    | The buffer is as it was saved or read.                            |
| [*]    | You have changed it and not yet saved it.                         |
| [%]    | The buffer is read-only. Typing does not change it. ~C-x C-q~ switches that. |

*** Words about the buffer

Just before the buffer's name, Tuatara may add a word that is lit up in a
colour of its own.

| Word      | Meaning                                                                                                       |
|-----------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Safe      | The file was opened safely. Tuatara shows its text and leaves the file untouched.                             |
| Evidence  | The file is in a directory listed in =*evidence-directories*=. It is never written to.                        |
| Narrow    | The buffer shows only a part of its text (~C-x x n~, ~C-x x s~). ~C-x x w~ shows all of it again.             |
| Look only | The buffer belongs to a git repository opened for looking at only. Commands that would change it are refused. |

*** The name, the line and the column

The buffer's name follows. When it is long, it is cut in the middle and a
mark stands for what is left out, down to 12 columns. =*mode-line-name-limit*=
sets a shorter limit.

After the name comes =L= and the line number, then =C= and the column, which
counts from 0. A number above 999 has commas, as in =L100,000,001=. When Tuatara
is still counting the lines of a very large file you see =L...= instead, and
the number appears when the count is done. The same holds for a column on a
line too long to measure at once.

After them, if the buffer has a major mode, comes its name, such as Org or
Terminal. A major mode is the set of habits Tuatara gives a buffer according to
what it holds: how it colours text, which keys it adds, and so on.

*** Other things the mode line says

While keys are recorded, the word Rec stands before the signs (~C-x (~
starts recording). While recorded keys play over and over for more than a
second, =Played 1,200/10,000= says how many plays are done. The words below
follow the mode name, each when it applies.

** The echo area and the messages log

The echo area is the last row of the frame. Tuatara writes a short message
there after most things you do, such as =Wrote /home/you/letter.txt=, and the
message stays until the next key you press does something. It also shows the
keys of a long sequence while you are typing it, and asks questions that one
key answers, such as yes or no.

Every message is also kept in the =*Messages*= buffer. A message said twice in
a row is written once with a count, as in =Saved [3 times]=. The buffer is
read-only, and it holds the last 1000 lines. Messages said while a password or
other secret was at hand are shown but not kept.

~C-h e~ opens the log across the bottom of the frame, 30% of the height,
with the cursor in it. It is a read-only buffer, so ~q~ closes it. The log
is the place to look when a message went by too fast.

#+begin_try Try it, with a message gone by
1. Press ~C-x C-f~, open =~/shopping.txt=, press ~i~, type =tea= and press
   ~C-g~. Then press ~C-x C-s~. The echo area says that the file was written
   (on a file with no change it says that no changes need to be saved). The
   message disappears when you press the next key.
2. Press ~C-h e~. The log opens at the bottom with the message in its last
   line. Press ~q~ to close it.
3. Press ~C-h N~ to read the notifications Tuatara or its packages have given
   this session, for example when an agenda entry's time came.
#+end_try

** The minibuffer and its prompt

When a command needs something from you, a file name, a word to search for, a
yes or no, it asks in the minibuffer, a row at the bottom of the frame
(Figure 1.1). The words that ask are the prompt, such as =Find file:=, and
your answer follows it (Figure 1.3). The prompt is drawn in bold. A frame
that has a question open keeps its window text where it was, and its cursor
is drawn as an outline there while you type in the minibuffer.

#+caption: Figure 1.3. The parts of the minibuffer.
#+name: fig-minibuffer
#+begin_example
 3/12 Find file: ~/notes/sh
 ---- ---------- ----------
 count prompt    answer
#+end_example

Press ~RET~ to give the answer, or ~C-g~ or ~ESC~ to give up. Before the prompt
Tuatara may show a count, such as =3/12=, which means the third of twelve
choices. A search shows which match the cursor is at and how many there are.
=*/0= means nothing matches but what you typed can be taken, and =!/0= means it
cannot.

You edit the answer with keys much like the ones in a text buffer. These keys
work only while a question is open, so =M-x= does not list their commands.

| Modal | Non-modal                  | Command                           | What it does           |
|-------+----------------------------+-----------------------------------+------------------------|
| none  | ~RET~                      | =minibuffer-finish=               | Gives the answer, taking the selected choice if there is one. |
| none  | ~C-j~, ~M-RET~             | =minibuffer-submit-input=         | Gives the answer exactly as typed, whatever is selected. |
| none  | ~C-g~, ~ESC~               | =minibuffer-cancel=               | Gives up the question. |
| none  | ~DEL~, ~C-h~               | =minibuffer-backward-delete-char= | Deletes the character before the cursor. |
| none  | ~C-b~, ~<left>~            | =minibuffer-backward-char=        | Moves the cursor left. |
| none  | ~C-f~, ~<right>~           | =minibuffer-forward-char=         | Moves the cursor right. |
| none  | ~C-a~, ~<home>~            | =minibuffer-beginning=            | Moves to the start of the answer. |
| none  | ~C-e~, ~<end>~             | =minibuffer-end=                  | Moves to the end of the answer. |
| none  | ~C-k~                      | =minibuffer-kill-line=            | Deletes the answer after the cursor. |
| none  | ~C-u~                      | =minibuffer-clear=                | Clears the answer. The prompt stays. |
| none  | ~C-<backspace>~, ~M-DEL~   | =minibuffer-backward-kill-word=   | Deletes the word before the cursor. |
| none  | ~C-y~, ~C-V~, ~S-<insert>~ | =minibuffer-paste=                | Pastes the clipboard, or what you cut last. |
| none  | ~M-p~                      | =minibuffer-previous-history=     | Brings back an earlier answer to this question. |
| none  | ~M-n~                      | =minibuffer-next-history=         | Goes to a later answer. |

A question the editor thinks is risky, such as deleting a file, needs the
whole word yes or no and then ~RET~. A question whose answer is easy to undo
takes one key. A password is typed with stars shown, and is never kept.

A question that offers a text to start with selects it, so that typing
replaces it and ~RET~ takes it.

** The choices panel

When a question has a list of answers, Tuatara shows them in the panel above
the last row, and narrows them as you type. Completing means finishing your
answer from that list. Type any part of a name. Several words may be typed,
and a choice has to match all of them, in any order. The letters that matched
are coloured, one colour for each word.

#+attr_html: :screen t :ml 2 :pr 3 :signs PD
#+caption: Figure 1.3. Choosing a file. The prompt is above the choices.
#+begin_example
Mix the batter and let it rest.
 TUATARA[N] PD [-] pancakes.txt  L2 C0  Text  ~/notes/
  2/3  Find file: ~/notes/sh
  shed-plan.txt
  shopping.txt
  sheep-notes.org
#+end_example

The selected choice is drawn reversed. ~C-n~ or ~<down>~ select the next one and
~C-p~ or ~<up>~ the one before. Above the first one is the line you typed, when
what you typed can be taken as it is. ~TAB~ puts the selected choice into the
answer. For a file name it replaces the part after the last slash, and for a
directory it goes into the directory, so that you can walk down a path with
~TAB~. ~RET~ gives the selected choice, and ~C-j~ gives what you typed.

At most 10 rows are used for the panel, the prompt row included. When there
are more choices, ~M-t~ makes the panel as tall as the frame allows, and
again as usual. A choice may
have a note beside it, such as the keys of a command, the mode of a buffer or
the permissions of a file. A title with a line beside it begins a group, such
as the directory that the files under it are in.

#+begin_try Try it, with a directory of notes
1. Press ~C-x C-f~. Tuatara starts where you chose last and lists what is there.
2. Type =~/= and look at the panel. Each choice is a file or a directory.
3. Type a few letters of a file's name. The list shrinks.
4. Press ~C-n~ twice and ~C-p~ once. The marked line moves.
5. Press ~TAB~. The name is put into the answer. If it was a directory, its
   contents are listed.
6. Press ~M-t~. The panel grows. Press it again to shrink it.
7. Press ~C-g~ to give up.
#+end_try

** Key hints

Press ~C-x~ and wait. The panel lists every key that can follow, with the
name of its command, so you do not have to remember them. Names that start
with a plus sign, such as =+rectangles and registers=, are further prefixes,
and pressing their key opens another list. A key whose command cannot run in
this buffer is dimmed, and pressing it says why. ~C-h~ right after a prefix
key lists every key under it in a buffer of its own. ~C-g~ closes the hints.

The hints take as many rows as the frame has room for, and the echo area
stays under them. If they do not all fit, the last row ends in =... C-h lists
all=.

#+begin_try Try it, with the keys under C-x

2. Press ~5~. The panel changes to the keys under ~C-x 5~, the ones for frames.
3. Press ~C-g~. The hints close and nothing was run.
4. Press ~C-x~ and then ~C-h~. A buffer lists all the keys under ~C-x~.
#+end_try

** The cursor, the gutter and header lines

The cursor is drawn as a block as standard. A bar or an underline can be
chosen, and each modal state may have its own shape and colour, so that you
see at once whether you are in NORMAL or INSERT state. In a window that does
not get the typing the cursor is drawn as an outline in a window of its own
and as an underline in a terminal.

The gutter is on the left edge of a window. It is empty as standard. Line
numbers appear in it when you ask: ~M-x toggle-line-numbers~ goes through none,
absolute and relative, where relative numbers show how many lines away each
line is. Where a long line wraps, the gutter shows a mark beside the extra
rows.

Tuatara has no tab bar. Buffers are reached by name (~C-x b~) or from the list
(~C-x C-b~). A window can have a header line at its top. A file of records,
such as a CSV, can keep its first row, the header, at the top of the window
while the rest scrolls under it.
